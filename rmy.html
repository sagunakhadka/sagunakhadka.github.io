What is the Ramayana?

The “journey of Rama,” told in Sanskrit verse.

The Ramayana is an ancient Sanskrit epic attributed to the sage Valmiki. It follows Prince Rama of Ayodhya: his banishment, the abduction of his wife Sita by the demon-king Ravana, the war to rescue her, and his return to become king. Beneath the adventure, it asks a lasting question: how do you do the right thing when duties pull in different directions?

Valmiki’s text is called the Adi Kavya, the “first poem.” Over the centuries it has been retold in nearly every language and art form across Asia.

Chapter by chapter

The Seven Kandas

Tap a chapter to read its story. Each one builds on the last.

1Bala KandaThe Book of Childhood

The story

Sage Narada tells Valmiki about an ideal man, and Valmiki is inspired to compose the epic. King Dasharatha of Ayodhya, childless, performs a sacrifice and is blessed with four sons: Rama, Bharata, Lakshmana and Shatrughna. The sage Vishwamitra takes young Rama and Lakshmana to protect his rituals from demons. Later, at King Janaka’s court in Mithila, Rama bends the great bow of Shiva and marries Sita.

Why it matters

It introduces the cast and shows Rama’s character early: obedient, brave and gentle.

2Ayodhya KandaThe Book of Ayodhya

The story

Dasharatha prepares to crown Rama. Queen Kaikeyi, swayed by her maid Manthara, claims two boons the king once promised her: Bharata to be king, and Rama exiled for fourteen years. Bound by his word, Dasharatha is heartbroken. Rama accepts at once. Sita and Lakshmana insist on going with him. Dasharatha dies of grief. Bharata refuses the throne, finds Rama in the forest, and rules as his regent, placing Rama’s sandals on the throne.

Why it matters

This is the emotional heart of the epic: promises, family loyalty and sacrifice. It also contains the tragic story of Shravana (see the lesser-known facts below).

3Aranya KandaThe Book of the Forest

The story

Rama, Sita and Lakshmana live in the Dandaka forest, meeting sages and protecting them from demons. Shurpanakha, Ravana’s sister, is rejected by Rama and Lakshmana and is humiliated. Ravana, seeking revenge, sends Maricha as a golden deer. Rama chases it, a false cry for help is heard, and Lakshmana leaves Sita to follow. Ravana appears as an ascetic and carries Sita off. The vulture Jatayu fights him and is mortally wounded.

4Kishkindha KandaThe Book of the Vanara Kingdom

The story

Searching for Sita, Rama meets Hanuman and then Sugriva, an exiled vanara king. They form an alliance: Rama will help Sugriva regain his kingdom from his brother Vali, and Sugriva will help find Sita. After Vali’s fall, Sugriva sends search parties in all directions. Hanuman, with Angada and Jambavan, learns from the vulture Sampati that Sita is held in Lanka.

5Sundara KandaThe Book of Beauty

The story

Hanuman leaps across the sea, enters Lanka, and finds Sita in the Ashoka grove, guarded and steadfast. He gives her Rama’s ring, and she sends back a jewel as a token. He is captured, his tail is set on fire, and he uses it to burn much of Lanka before returning with the news.

Why it matters

Hanuman’s courage and devotion make this a favourite chapter, and many people recite it for strength in hard times.

6Yuddha KandaThe Book of War

The story

The vanara army builds a bridge to Lanka. Ravana’s brother Vibhishana, urging peace, joins Rama. In the great war, Lakshmana is struck down and revived by herbs Hanuman brings. Ravana’s son Indrajit and brother Kumbhakarna fall, and finally Rama defeats Ravana. Sita is reunited with Rama and, as the text tells it, proves her purity by passing through fire. They return to Ayodhya after fourteen years, and Rama is crowned.

7Uttara KandaThe Book of the Aftermath

The story

This final book looks back at Ravana’s origins and covers Rama’s reign, Sita’s later exile, the birth of her twin sons Lava and Kusha at Valmiki’s ashram, and Rama’s departure from the world.

A note

Many scholars regard this book as a later addition, and it remains the most debated part of the epic, especially its treatment of Sita.

Who’s who

Key Characters

Who’s who in the epic.

Rama

Prince of Ayodhya, honoured as Maryada Purushottam, the ideal of right conduct. Regarded as an avatar of Vishnu.

Sita

Princess of Mithila, Rama’s wife. Known for courage, resolve and dignity.

Lakshmana

Rama’s younger brother, fiercely loyal, who shares the whole exile.

Hanuman

The mighty vanara devotee of Rama, a symbol of strength and service.

Ravana

Learned and powerful king of Lanka, undone by pride and desire.

Bharata

Rama’s brother who refuses the throne he was given and governs in Rama’s name.

Dasharatha

King of Ayodhya and father of the four princes.

Kaikeyi

Dasharatha’s queen whose two boons set the exile in motion.

Vibhishana

Ravana’s righteous brother, who sides with Rama.

One story, many voices

Many Ramayanas

The story travelled far and changed as it went.

Valmiki Ramayana

The original Sanskrit epic.

Ramcharitmanas

Tulsidas’s Awadhi retelling, hugely popular in North India.

Kamban’s Ramavataram

A Tamil classic from South India.

Bhanubhakta Ramayana

The first major Nepali-language Ramayana, a landmark in Nepali literature.

Ramakien

The Thai national epic, based on the Ramayana.

Jain and Buddhist tellings

Versions that reinterpret the characters through their own traditions.

From the text

Lesser-known facts

Sita was no passive bystander

The source stresses that Sita is a strong, outspoken figure. She insisted on joining Rama in exile, urged Lakshmana to go after Rama when she heard the false cry, and after the war answered Rama’s words with the fire ordeal on her own terms.

Sita’s character

Rama’s many roles

The article argues that Rama should be seen as son, brother, king and husband, not just as Sita’s husband, and that confusion arises when we mix these roles. It also points to Rama’s deep grief when Sita was taken: weeping, collapsing and calling her name before the vanaras.

Rama and Sita

Rama and Sita as equals

The author quotes Swami Vivekananda, who describes Rama as the Absolute and Sita as its Power. In his retelling, Sita says that at their wedding fire she felt she belonged to Rama and he to her, each completing the other’s life.

Partnership

Women with real roles

According to the source, Kaikeyi fought beside Dasharatha in battle, Kausalya performed the morning fire ritual (Agnihotra), and Sita carried out daily religious duties. It also claims that Vashishtha suggested Sita could stay and rule instead of going to the forest. This last point is the author’s claim, so check it against the Sanskrit text.

Women in the epic

Everyone invited to the Ashvamedha

The article cites verses describing the horse sacrifice, where people of all four varnas were invited and told to be treated with respect.

Society

The sage-boy Shravana

The youth accidentally killed by Dasharatha is described as a hermit in bark clothing and deerskin, devoted to study and austerity. By his own words (Ayodhya Kanda 2.63.53) his father was a Vaishya and his mother a Shudra. The source highlights that Valmiki mentions this in passing, as nothing unusual.

Ayodhya Kanda 2.63–64

A curse without Brahmahatya

Shravana’s father tells Dasharatha that, because the killing was unknowing, he would not bear the sin of killing a Brahmin. The source reads this as showing that spiritual worth rests on conduct rather than birth. It adds that Shravana was said to ascend to heaven alongside Indra.

Karma over birth

Rama’s respect for others’ mothers

The article quotes Sita praising Rama as someone who treats every woman Dasharatha has even glanced upon as his own mother.
